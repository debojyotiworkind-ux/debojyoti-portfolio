Interactive Museum Installation

Not every project starts with a camera.
This one started with a blank room and a national postal history to tell.

India Post commissioned a complete reimagining of how their history is experienced — moving from static plaques to an interactive, multi-sensory museum. The brief covered everything: spatial layout, exhibit wall art, character design, and the video content that would bring each era of Indian postal history to life. This was ideation-to-execution work at a national scale — every wall, every mascot expression, every transition between exhibits was designed from scratch.

Exhibit Walls

Wall art, panel by panel

Six exhibit walls, each one tracing a different chapter of India's postal history — from the earliest hand-struck stamps to the color-coded letter boxes still standing on Indian streets today.

"Prathama Tikaten / First Stamps" — from the 1775 Bishop Mark to the Scinde Dawk and the Inverted Head Four Annas error.
Exhibit Wall

British India —
1855 to 1922.

A full timeline wall spanning the Perforated East India Issues through to the Prince of Wales Camp Postmarks — bilingual captioning in Hindi and English, designed to be read at a walking pace.

Exhibit Wall

The people
behind the post.

An interactive cube panel profiling the department's notable figures — from Ganendra Prosad Roy, India's first Director General of Posts, to Dr. Sushila Chaurasia, the first woman to head a Postal Circle.

"Patra Petiyan / Letter Boxes" — red, blue, green, and yellow explained · "Dak Parivahan / Mail Transportation" — plane, van, cycle, and rail in one mural.
"Sandeshe Aate Hai" — a listening bar with gramophone and radio, honoring songs written about letters and longing.
Character Design

The mascot.

Ideation

A letter box,
brought to life.

The museum needed a face — something a child could recognize instantly and an adult could smile at. The mascot design turns India Post's own red letter box into a friendly, walking character in postman uniform, phone in hand, ready to guide visitors through the exhibits.
