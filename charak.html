Charak —
The Festival

West Bengal, India
Annual Festival
16 Photographs
Scroll
The Story

One of Bengal's most intense religious festivals — captured up close, frame by frame, as devotion turns physical.

Charak Puja unfolds at the edge of risk. Devotees are bound by rope and hoisted from a tall wooden pole, spinning over rooftops as crowds gather below — cameras and phones raised, breath held. What follows is a sequence of sixteen frames, moving from preparation to flight to the quiet after — an attempt to hold a moment that, by nature, refuses to stay still.

The leap — a devotee swings out over the crowd, saffron robes catching the evening light.
Chapter One

The crowd
gathers.

Long before the first swing, the street fills. Families climb to rooftops, vendors thread through the press of bodies, and a quiet anticipation builds — the kind reserved for things that happen only once a year, in exactly this place.

Offerings clutched tight — sweets and coins bound for the watching crowd below. · Sweets scatter mid-air as the rope swings wide over rooftops and wires.
"

Faith here isn't quiet. It is loud, physical, and witnessed — by hundreds of raised phones and a street that refuses to look away.

— On photographing Charak
Chapter Two

The binding —
ritual before risk.

Before any devotee is lifted, the ropes are wound with deliberate care. Helpers tie knot after knot around the waist, the rhythm almost meditative — a quiet counterweight to the spectacle that follows.

Faces before the climb — devotion rendered in vibhuti ash, beads, and stillness.
Chapter Three

Suspended —
between earth and sky.

This is the moment the festival is named for: a body lifted clean off the ground, held only by rope and the strength of arms that have done this before. Below, hundreds watch in a single shared breath.

Chapter Three · The Swing

Mid-air —
held by faith.

The crowd below holds hundreds of raised phones, each trying to freeze what the eye cannot hold. Up there, surrounded by noise and sky, something else entirely is happening — a body in motion, a mind completely still.

Chapter Four

After —
the quiet return.

When the rope finally lowers, there is no applause, no fanfare — just devotees stepping back into the crowd that watched them fly. The street absorbs them again, ordinary and unremarkable, until next year.
